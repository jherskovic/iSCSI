# NVMe/TCP soak, 2026-09-01 → 02

Six hours of verified mixed I/O against the NAS through the shipping stack,
the first long run of the NVMe/TCP path. **Clean: 23 cycles, 3,544,211
SHA-256-verified reads, 0 errors, 0 connection losses, memory flat.**

## Setup

- Initiator: the SIP-off VM at 192.168.0.44 (macOS 26.6.2, 16 GiB, 4 CPUs,
  bridged NIC), running the `nvme-of` branch's app and daemon (Debug build,
  write-through default). Attached with `mount -F` on an `nvme://` URL,
  `hdiutil` CRawDiskImage, a fresh APFS volume — the path the app takes.
- Target: TrueNAS SCALE 25.10 `nvmet` at 192.168.20.1:4420, subsystem
  `…:name-testing`, namespace 1 (512 GiB zvol, 512-byte blocks), both
  digests on, in-capsule data 16 KiB, VWC present; the daemon logged
  `write cache ENABLED, write-through (FUA)`.
- Driver: `scripts/vm-nvme-soak.sh`, detached on the VM. 20:24 to 02:33.

Each ~16-minute cycle: `scripts/soak.py` (4 workers, 15 min of unaligned
read-modify-write and whole-file rewrites, every read verified; 2 GiB of
memory pressure on even cycles), a 1 GiB sequential write with `sync`,
`purge` and checksummed read-back, 64 fsync'd 1 MiB files written and
verified, and a live `diskutil verifyVolume`. The daemon log was captured
throughout and the RSS of `iscsid`, the extension process and `fskitd`
sampled every minute.

## What the numbers mean, and what they do not

- The ~50 MB/s sequential ceiling is the bridged virtual NIC, not the path:
  the same daemon read 116 MB/s from the VM in `read-bench`, and loopback
  against the simulator does 1.3 GB/s. Cycle 1's 91 MB/s read came from a
  cold volume; the 22 to 36 MB/s writes in cycles 5 to 7 coincided with the
  no-pressure verified-read count dipping and recovering (197k → 158k →
  160k), which reads as the shared NAS being busier for half an hour, not
  as the initiator degrading: throughput within each cycle was flat and
  memory never moved.
- Memory pressure costs about 10% of verified reads and nothing else.
- Not exercised here: any fault. Six hours without a single connection loss
  is the right result against a healthy target, and it means the recovery
  path ran zero times. That path is covered by the crash and recovery
  suites and by `scripts/vm-nvme-verify.sh`, not by this soak.
- One target, one namespace, one VM. Item 3 of `open-questions.md` applies
  to NVMe/TCP exactly as it does to iSCSI.

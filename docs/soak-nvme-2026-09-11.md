# NVMe/TCP soak on a live HFS+ namespace, 2026-09-11

The second NVMe/TCP soak, and the first with three things the September 1 run
did not have: the **released 0.6.0 build** rather than a development one, a
**namespace with existing data** that had to survive untouched, and the
**SIP-on acceptance VM** (`herko@192.168.0.37`, 2 vCPU, 8 GiB) rather than the
4-core SIP-off rig. It is also the first soak to compare **FUA on every write**
against the **5-second flush timer** on the same volume.

Not a performance result. Read "What the network did" before any number.

## Setup

- Target: TrueNAS SCALE 25.10 `nvmet`, subsystem `…:ssd-vms`, namespace 1: a
  **bare journaled HFS+ volume** written straight onto the 215 GiB namespace
  (no partition map), 37% full, holding one UTM VM bundle. Write cache present.
- Path: `mount -F -t iSCSI nvme://192.168.20.1:4420/<nqn>/1`, `hdiutil attach
  … CRawDiskImage -nomount`, then `diskutil mount` of the existing volume.
  `scripts/vm-nvme-soak.sh` grew a `FORMAT=0` mode for this: no erase, the
  volume mounted as is, every write under one dated directory, only that
  directory removed at the end. The UTM bundle was byte-for-byte untouched.
- Cycles as on September 1: 15 min of SHA-256-verified mixed I/O by 4 workers
  (2 GiB of memory pressure on even cycles), a 1 GiB sequential write with
  purge and verified read-back, 64 fsync'd 1 MiB files verified, and a live
  `diskutil verifyVolume` of the HFS+ volume.
- The VM reaches the NAS through the home router (192.168.0.1 → 192.168.20.1),
  not the dev Mac's direct 10 GbE link. Idle, that path read 106–117 MB/s raw
  (`iscsictl nvme read-bench`, queue depth 1–8) at 08:30.

## Results

FUA on every write (`writeThrough`, the default), 08:36–09:14:

| cycle | pressure | verified reads | RMW ops | seq write MB/s | seq read MB/s | fsync 64 | verifyVolume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | – | 70,296 | 32,803 | 12.2 | 11.1 | ok | OK |
| 2 | 2 GiB | 73,444 | 34,266 | 12.8 | 49.4 | ok | OK |

Flush timer, 5 s (`flushIntervalSeconds: 5`), 09:15–10:08, same volume:

| cycle | pressure | verified reads | RMW ops | seq write MB/s | seq read MB/s | fsync 64 | verifyVolume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | – | 155,430 | 72,537 | 16.5 | 9.9 | ok | OK |
| 2 | 2 GiB | 159,656 | 74,509 | 21.1 | 11.2 | ok | OK |
| 3 | – | 102,826 ¹ | 47,984 | 43.6 | 94.4 | ok | OK |

¹ Cycle 3 lost about three minutes of its 15 to diagnostics: the workload was
paused (SIGSTOP) for 2.5 min to measure the raw transport with the NAS
drained, and two `read-bench` runs shared the link. Cycle 2 of the FUA run and
cycle 2 of the timer run also shared the 2 vCPUs with short probes.

Across all five cycles: **zero verification errors, zero connection losses,
zero recoveries, zero daemon errors**, every fsync set verified, every live
volume check clean. The run was stopped after timer cycle 3 by request, to
re-home the VM's network; it was not a failure.

Memory (RSS, KB): `iscsid` 21→35 (max 37), extension 39→40 (max 50), `fskitd`
23→8 over the FUA run; `iscsid` 7→35 (max 35), extension 40 (max 50) over the
timer run. Same shape as September 1, no growth.

## What the flush timer bought

Same volume, same VM, same degraded network (below), the timer **doubled the
verified small-file work per cycle** (70k → 155k reads, 33k → 73k
read-modify-write patches). The soak's writes are 512 B to 1 MiB with
unaligned patches, so under FUA each one is a synchronous commit on the zvol;
the timer turns them into cached writes flushed every 5 s. The sequential write
moved only 12 → 16–21 MB/s, because that step was bounded by the network
during those cycles, not by FUA.

## What the network did

The sequential columns of the first four timer-and-FUA cycles are not the
initiator's. Between 08:36 and about 10:00 the VM's path to the NAS forwarded
at roughly a tenth of its idle rate, and it was measured every way available:

| measurement | result |
|---|---|
| raw `read-bench`, VM → NAS, 08:30 (idle) | 106–117 MB/s |
| raw `read-bench`, VM → NAS, 09:50–09:59, workload paused 40 s | **11.8 MB/s**, ssd-vms and name-testing identical |
| raw `read-bench`, dev Mac → NAS over its direct 10 GbE | 607 MB/s (same moment) |
| VM → dev Mac over the VM bridge (TCP, Python sender) | 711 MB/s |
| ICMP through the router: 200-packet burst, 1472-byte don't-fragment | 0% loss, ~1 ms |
| VM TCP counters over 10 s of soak traffic | 0 retransmits, 0 out-of-order, 0 dup ACKs |
| HFS+ → image device during the sequential step (`fs_usage`) | 2 MiB reads every ~175 ms |
| CPU during the sequential step | `iscsid` 12%, plenty idle |
| raw `read-bench`, VM → NAS, 10:08 (cycle 4's small-file phase running) | **102 MB/s** |

So: the NAS and its pools were fine, the VM's NIC was fine, nothing was lost
or fragmented, and the initiator was idle-waiting. The segment between the
router and the NAS forwarded slowly for about 80 minutes and then did not.
It recovered within minutes of the workload pause at 09:57, which may be
cause or coincidence. Writes suffered less than reads throughout (21–44 MB/s
against 10–12) — the direction that returns fewer packets per byte.

Two false leads, recorded so they are not chased again:

- The extension's `avgReq=16 KiB` is cumulative over the mount and dominated by
  the small-file phase. On the local `proto` store, DiskImages hands the
  extension up to 1 MiB requests on **both** HFS+ and APFS (355 / 444 MB/s
  write, no network), so HFS+ under DiskImages is not a 16 KiB fragmenter.
- A 35 KB asset from the NAS web UI takes 5.4 s through the router at all
  times, including after the NVMe path recovered, and 10 ms over the direct
  link. That is the UI's TLS setup on that path, not a transport symptom.

One unexplained transient: a probe reading the UTM bundle's 83 GB image
through the mounted volume got `ENOENT` at the cycle 2 boundary of the FUA
run; the file was present and unmodified (mtime Sep 2) before and after.

## What it establishes

- The shipping 0.6.0 build attaches an existing HFS+ volume over NVMe/TCP and
  survives five cycles of verified mixed I/O on it, on the acceptance rig,
  with nothing on the volume disturbed. The non-destructive soak mode is
  reusable for any live namespace whose contents must survive.
- The 5 s flush timer is worth about 2× on small-file workloads against this
  target; the crash-consistency pair (September 1) already bounds what it
  costs on a power cut.
- The rig itself caps the per-cycle counts: 2 vCPUs ran at a load average of
  2.7 during the small-file phase. September 1's 197k verified reads per cycle
  came from 4 cores, so the two soaks are not comparable on that column.
- Throughput was network-bound and not measured. A rerun with the VM bridged
  onto the dev Mac's direct 10 GbE link is the way to get the numbers.
